                    ProtoObjectTest etc. in the SUnit suite.

  ### What this confirms

  - JIT helps modestly (+2 to +10 %) on send-heavy or
    primitive-light workloads where the inline IC + JITted
    prologue earn back their cost (fib, sieve, sum, dict).
  - JIT breaks even (±0 %) on workloads dominated by primitives
    (allocation, getter+yourself, block-only loops).
  - JIT hurts (-6 %) on heap-walking / megamorphic workloads
    (sort comparison sends are polymorphic across the heap).
  - Cog is **4–34× faster** across the board.  The gap is
    largest on send-heavy IC-friendly code (fib 34×, sends/sec
    27×, getter 23×) — exactly the territory where Cog's full
    JIT, register allocation, and method inlining pay off.
    Smallest on LargeInteger-heavy work (factorial 6×) where the
    primitive does the heavy lifting in both VMs.
  - Closing the Cog gap requires architectural work: method
    inlining (Sista phase 4+), register allocator in T2, true
    PIC with code patching, shrinking stencils from ~460 B/bc
    to Cog-comparable ~80 B/bc.  Incremental T1 tuning won't
    move the 17–34× headline.
